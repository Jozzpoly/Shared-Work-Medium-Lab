# Peripheral Candidate Generation Probe — 2026-10-07

**Status:** EXPERIMENTAL FIXTURE · UNRUN · DRAFT-BRANCH ONLY · NOT PROJECT AUTHORITY

## Research question

Can a sparse, source-linked shared event surface make a materially relevant **other project/world enter an agent's candidate set** during ordinary non-SWM work, without the Owner naming the donor and without a central capability registry?

This is deliberately different from routing.

- **candidate generation**: does the relevant other world occur to the agent at all?
- **routing**: once candidate worlds exist, which one should the agent inspect?

Earlier pinned-candidate experiments can study routing. They cannot establish open-world candidate generation.

## Why Slack is currently interesting

The live field run exposed several useful properties:

- quiet top-level/thread presence;
- time-bounded delta reads;
- source links and stable message handles;
- queryable reaction facets;
- direct dereference after shallow discovery;
- a 90-day searchable Free-plan horizon that may act as natural peripheral decay;
- a clear separation between project truth and Slack projection.

Slack is the current field substrate, not the claimed architecture.

## Required subject boundary

The subject must begin with a **real goal that is not about testing SWM**.

Do not tell the subject:
- that a donor exists;
- which projects are candidates;
- that it should search cross-project history;
- that Slack contains the answer;
- that it is participating in a Medium experiment.

A subject already deeply contaminated by the planted relation cannot qualify the primary result.

## Stimulus construction

Use a real source-bound event from another project.

A scent may contain:
- a short factual observation or bounded finding;
- its originating project/lens;
- an exact public source door;
- explicit scope/anti-claim when needed.

A scent must **not** contain:
- "this is relevant to project X";
- global importance/relevance scores;
- a precomputed target project;
- a universal ontology;
- copied project state;
- a claim stronger than the source supports.

The emitter may assert only local materiality:
> something worth making peripherally reachable happened here.

The receiving mind decides relevance.

## Conditions

### C0 — ordinary environment

The subject receives its normal project-local task and **all ordinary tools it would normally possess**.

For Browser ChatGPT this explicitly includes existing Opera context such as open tabs and recent browser history when those sensors are normally available. Do not cripple the baseline to make Slack win.

No additional purpose-built shared scent surface is exposed or mentioned.

### C0a — implicit working-set scent (diagnostic, when measurable)

Observe whether the subject spontaneously uses naturally occurring environmental traces already produced by ordinary work, such as:
- open browser tabs;
- recent browser history;
- current project/sidebar topology;
- recently visited project Pages, CI runs or source surfaces.

This is not a separate product treatment if those sensors are already part of C0. It is a diagnostic attribution:
> did candidate generation come from Owner-created ambient traces before any Shared Medium scent was needed?

### C1 — sparse explicit peripheral scent

Same task and ordinary tools, including the normal working-set sensors above.

A small recent Slack/shared-medium delta additionally contains:
- one or a few real source-linked scents;
- at least one materially relevant donor event;
- unrelated recent scents only when they are ecologically legitimate, not artificial camouflage.

The subject is not instructed to inspect them.

### C2 — saturated peripheral surface

Use only if C1 first demonstrates value.

Same task and tools, but expose a materially denser event window containing many low-value or superficially related traces.

Purpose:
- test whether candidate-generation gain collapses under social-information saturation;
- measure false donor activation and reading burden;
- falsify the assumption that "more shared awareness is better."

Do not manufacture absurd spam. Saturation should resemble plausible multi-project churn.

## Peripheral sensor contract

For the current Slack connector, distinguish sensing modes:

- channel delta / timeline: peripheral new-event sensor;
- search: locator, not current truth;
- reaction facets: quiet handling metadata;
- exact message/thread read: foveal inspection;
- public source door: authority verification.

When app/bot/webhook emitters are involved, explicitly include bot-origin events in the sensor. The current Slack search connector defaults to `include_bots=false`; a bot-emitted scent may otherwise exist in the medium but be invisible to search.

Do not assume semantic search. The current connector explicitly reports semantic search unavailable for this user.

## Passive ecological measurement plane

When the subject uses the same Opera environment, the evaluator may use browser-history **deltas** as a low-interference behavioral trace.

Before the run:
- snapshot the recent history set;
- predeclare the experiment-relevant source-door URLs/domains that are allowed to be inspected for scoring.

After the run:
- compute only the delta relevant to those declared sources;
- record whether the subject traversed from the ambient/peripheral surface into an authoritative donor source;
- correlate that with any durable local effect such as a repo change, experiment result, or source-bound decision.

This can provide evidence for:

`scent exposure -> source traversal -> local effect`

without:
- asking the subject to self-report Medium use;
- requiring chain-of-thought;
- instrumenting every donor project with analytics.

Privacy boundary:
- do **not** treat the Owner's general browser history as a research corpus;
- ignore unrelated URLs even if the connector returns them;
- report only aggregate/relevant experiment paths needed for the claim;
- history proves navigation, not comprehension or causality.

## Primary measures

Record:

1. **candidate-set entry**
   - did the correct other world/project appear in the subject's reasoning or source path?

2. **spontaneity**
   - did this happen without Owner or experiment prompt naming the donor or Medium?

3. **source verification**
   - did the subject follow the scent to the authoritative/local project source before using it?

4. **material transfer**
   - did the donor change a decision, experiment, falsifier, implementation, or understanding in the subject's own task?

5. **false activation**
   - how many irrelevant worlds/scents were investigated?

6. **attention / acquisition cost**
   - extra reads/hops;
   - derailment from local work;
   - whether the subject ignored the medium appropriately.

7. **claim discipline**
   - did the subject preserve donor scope and local authority rather than importing an analogy as architecture?

## Strong PASS candidate

A strong result looks like:

> An agent pursuing a non-SWM project goal, without a supplied donor list or Owner route, notices a recent source-linked scent, enters the donor project's own truth surface, extracts a bounded transferable result, and materially improves its local work. It ignores unrelated scents and does not promote Slack into project authority.

One episode is still not infrastructure proof.

Repeated instrumental use across different receiving projects/bodies would be much stronger evidence.

## Important valid outcome: NONE

Ignoring all scents can be the correct behavior.

If the local project already contains the needed candidate set, or no foreign donor materially improves the decision, Medium should remain silent/unused.

The live Feniks and ReflexBrain dogfood on 2026-10-07 produced exactly this bounded negative result: the currently available Slack signals did not add material decision value beyond those projects' already-active local context.

This is evidence **against** always-on global routing.

## Falsifiers

The candidate-generation hypothesis weakens materially if:

- C1 does not improve candidate-set entry over the real ordinary environment, including Opera working-set traces when available;
- open tabs/history/current project surfaces already provide the same useful candidate at comparable or lower cost;
- the relevant donor was already easily discoverable through ordinary project-native sources;
- subjects follow scents because their formatting advertises experimental importance;
- C1 mainly increases archaeology or analogy hunting;
- Slack/social traces override local source authority;
- subjects begin treating every similarity as a donor;
- the system requires global relevance scoring or target-project assignment to work;
- C2 performs as well as or better than C1 solely because "more context" was added;
- Owner routing remains necessary at the material decision boundary.

## Existing-environment counterpressure

A live Opera inspection during fixture design exposed a strong competing substrate:

- open tabs reveal the Owner's immediate working set;
- up to seven days of browser history reveal recent project/world visits;
- visited URLs can expose specific experiment surfaces, CI runs and project Pages without the Owner writing any handoff.

This is a form of human-produced environmental trace: ordinary work leaves a recency-weighted scent that an agent may be able to use for candidate generation.

It is **not authority** and may contain unrelated/private/noisy activity.

Therefore a Shared Medium layer earns its role only when it adds useful cross-world reachability beyond this naturally occurring working-set scent.

## Saturation / decay interpretation

Stigmergic coordination offers useful prior art: independent agents can coordinate indirectly through environmental traces rather than direct addressing.

It also warns about positive-feedback lock-in and environmental saturation.

For SWM the attractive hypothesis is therefore not:
> make every project maximally visible.

It is:
> let locally material traces become briefly peripherally reachable, then decay unless real receiving work gives them new life.

Durable knowledge remains in project sources.

# Hot vs cold candidate generation — 2026-10-08

**Status:** source-bound working-set calibration · research distinction · not architecture.

## Question

Can one peripheral/recency mechanism reasonably solve both:

1. **hot candidate generation** — noticing another world that is already active in the current working environment;
2. **cold precedent retrieval** — recovering a materially relevant older/dormant donor that is not currently active?

Live Opera working-set evidence says: probably not.

Therefore:

> recent working-set scent is useful evidence about **what is active**, not complete evidence about **what is relevant**.

A dormant donor can be semantically more relevant than a high-churn active project.

## Working distinction

### Hot / peripheral candidate generation

Problem:
> something relevant is happening now in another world; will it enter the receiver's candidate set?

Promising sensors:
- source-native project activity;
- open tabs;
- recent browser history;
- sparse Slack/shared events when the source lacks a sufficient native signal;
- direct messages / cross-tool events.

Freshness matters strongly.
Decay is useful.
Quiet Presence / attention-sovereignty constraints apply.

### Cold / precedent candidate generation

Problem:
> a materially similar problem happened before in a world that is not currently active; will that precedent enter the candidate set?

Recency alone is structurally insufficient.

Candidate mechanisms under research:
- historical transfer cases;
- source-bound decision-pressure corpus;
- cheap lexical/structural candidate generation followed by source verification;
- ephemeral relational scent derived from prior transfer episodes;
- transactive knowledge of where a kind of experience may live.

Freshness still matters at reuse time, but the historical case itself need not be recent.

## Critical non-collapse

Do not solve cold precedent retrieval by:
- continuously mirroring every repository event;
- keeping every project hot;
- notifying the Owner about dormant donors;
- building a universal capability registry;
- ranking all projects globally by recency or importance.

Do not solve hot peripheral awareness by:
- reading a giant historical case corpus every turn;
- forcing cross-project archaeology at every local result;
- treating every active project as relevant.

These are different expected-value regimes.

## Relation to existing Medium research

This distinction connects two previously separate lines:

- **PR #10 / Peripheral Candidate Generation:** hot-world candidate entry through ambient/source-native/shared traces.
- **Decision Pressure / case-memory work:** cold precedent retrieval when novelty/absence/escalation or another material boundary justifies historical search.

A future system may combine them, but experiments should not collapse them.

## Strong falsifiers

The distinction weakens if:
- ordinary ambient traces reliably recover dormant but relevant donors at comparable cost;
- cold precedent retrieval provides no decision improvement over local reasoning;
- hot event sensing requires historical archaeology anyway;
- one simple source-native mechanism provides both high recall and low noise across the tested ecology.

## Next experimental consequence

The Peripheral Candidate Generation fixture should explicitly label a task as:

- **HOT** — donor has a real recent source-native/shared event;
- **COLD** — donor is intentionally absent from the current 7-day working set and must be recovered by precedent relevance rather than recency.

Do not compare Slack/Opera against a cold task and then conclude peripheral sensing failed; that would test the wrong mechanism.

Do not compare case-memory retrieval only on hot tasks and conclude it is the right always-on sensor; that would reward unnecessary archaeology.

No behavioral PASS is claimed here.

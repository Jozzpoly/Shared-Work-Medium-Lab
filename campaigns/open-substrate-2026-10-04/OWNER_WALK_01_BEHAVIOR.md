## Why this matters

Browser Walk 02 established that the surface was coherent enough to hand to Owner.

This recording is stronger interaction evidence because it shows what Owner actually chose to inspect when left with the preview.

It still does **not** establish the ecological success criterion because Owner entered the surface after being explicitly routed there.

The minimal identity-only object was not entered during the recorded walk.

## What the behavior supports

### 1. Progressive disclosure is no longer blocking depth

Owner did not stop at the landing page.

He repeatedly moved from:

human-facing surface
→ local/participant records
→ disclosure detail
→ raw JSON
→ technical view
→ back to human-facing surface.

That is real behavioral support for the layered presentation model.

It does **not** prove that the wording/design is pleasant or final.

### 2. Raw truth is not merely an implementation appendix

Owner voluntarily entered raw JSON and technical view more than once.

For this Owner, deep evidence access should remain a first-class voluntary path.

Do not simplify the Owner surface by deleting or distancing raw provenance merely to make the page look cleaner.

The useful pattern appears to be:

> calm entry + exact depth on demand

rather than:

> simple Owner page + inaccessible technical appendix.

### 3. The rich relational object attracted the exploration

The recorded walk concentrated entirely on the richer shared object.

The identity-only control was not entered.

Do not over-interpret this as dislike of the minimal object; it was an explicit control and had little content.

But there is no behavioral evidence that bare identity/fallback objects are themselves worth visiting.

The interesting surface is where a thing carries:

- content;
- source;
- independently owned local meanings;
- participant perspective;
- inspectable provenance.

### 4. Reversible navigation matters

Owner repeatedly descended and returned.

The useful interaction is not a one-way “read more” hierarchy.

It is closer to walking between layers of the same thing while preserving orientation.

### 5. No evidence of ecological adoption

This remains Level-1/Owner-routed evidence.

The recording must not be counted as:

- independent discovery;
- autonomous agent reuse;
- ecological Medium success;
- proof that another project would choose the object instrumentally.

## What the recording does not tell us

There is no spoken commentary.

Therefore it does not establish:

- whether Owner liked the visual design;
- whether the terminology was understandable;
- whether any click was caused by confusion rather than curiosity;
- whether the object felt useful rather than merely inspectable;
- whether Owner would return without being asked.

Do not infer emotional/product judgement from click depth alone.

## Combined judgement

The recording materially strengthens this narrow claim:

> **the current layered surface is traversable enough that further Browser-led UI polishing is not the highest-value next move.**

It does not strengthen the ecological claim.

## Frontier implication

Stop adding synthetic object varieties and stop polishing the generic viewer absent new concrete friction.

The next problem is not “can another shape be rendered?”

The next problem is:

> **can something produced for real work enter Medium as a truthful shared thing, become worth finding, and later be discovered instrumentally without Owner routing?**

That should be split into two pressures rather than solved at once:

1. **real-work admission** — admit one independently valuable object created for another project's own goal, preserving real source/provenance without forcing a new core type;
2. **ecological doorway** — only after such real material exists, expose a quiet, non-notifying route through which another workstream could discover it while pursuing its own goal.

Do not build search/recommendation before this simpler pressure fails.
